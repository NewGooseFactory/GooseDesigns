Eleven words, and the last two are the risk

Every methodology says it works. This one puts it in the description.

github.com/obra/superpowers

A framework and a methodology are both nouns you can inspect. That works is a verdict, and putting a verdict in your own description invites the reader to go and check it, which is a heavier bet than the two nouns in front of it.

The sentence, split into what you can inspect and what you have to test

all three quoted from the description
a framework

Files, structure, and an interface. You can read one and know what it expects of you without running anything.

inspect the repo
a methodology

An opinion about the order work should happen in. Also inspectable, and disagreeable on sight if the order is wrong for you.

read and disagree
that works

A verdict, stated by the author about their own project. Nothing in the description says on what, for whom, or measured how.

no evidence named
what would
settle it

A named project it was used on, a before and after, or somebody who is not the author saying the same thing. None of the three appears in the description, which leaves the strongest word in the sentence resting on the reader's willingness to try it.

Saying it out loud is a choice

Most frameworks leave the verdict to the reader. Writing it into your own line is either confidence or a shortcut, and the repo page is where a reader goes to find out which.
