Forensic tooling for investigators

Traces.
Not verdicts.

It gathers forensic traces from an acquisition and checks them against public indicators of compromise. What it cannot do is call a device clean.

From the project's own warnings

Traces worth investigatingForensic artifacts from an acquisition, matched against published indicators.
can show
Known campaign markersIndicators published by Amnesty International and other research groups.
cannot show
That a device is cleanThe project states plainly that public indicators are insufficient to determine a device was not targeted.

The third row is the reason this page exists: a tool that names its own blind spot is more useful than one that does not.
Reliance on public indicators alone can miss recent traces and give a false sense of security.

Who it is written for

Technologists and investigators

The project describes it as a forensic research tool requiring digital forensics knowledge and command-line work.

Not end-user self-assessment

Stated directly in the README, with a recommendation to seek expert assistance instead.

Supported by a helpline

Civil society support is routed through Amnesty International's Security Lab and a partner helpline.
